One scope, stated three times

Delete and export both use the page's filter and drill scope — the same rows every other page is showing. That was a deliberate reversal of an earlier design where delete carried its own hidden scope: two scoping rules on one page meant the count under the button never matched the count in the header. Now the scope trail restates the drill as pills, the delete card restates the active filters as read-only chips, and the volume table counts exactly that. Older than is the single delete-only refinement.

Why Export is the narrow one

It carries three buttons and no body, so an even third of the row is mostly empty card. It takes a quarter, clamped to 240–380px, and the two cards that do hold content take the surplus. The floor is the measured width of the button row — 196px of buttons and gaps plus 28px of padding — which is also why the labels are CSV and JSON rather than Export CSV (filtered): the card's heading and note already say both things, and the long forms pushed the three buttons onto two lines. The full phrasing survives as their tooltips. The same action in the header's data-source menu is still Save database copy: those buttons are stacked full-width with room to spare, and the longer name is the clearer one wherever it fits.

The bounds are not decoration. A first cut used a rounder-looking 300px floor, which beat the two fluid columns on any window under about 1500px and made the card with three buttons and no body the widest thing on the page. Below 1100px all three stack, and the binding constraint there is the Delete card's action row, never Export's.

The volume table

It groups by the level just below the deepest drilled one — repo → branch → task → agent_path, which is the floor — so drilling one step always answers "and what is that count made of?". Columns above the grouping level repeat the scope's value; columns below it read All, because the row aggregates every value they could take. Groups are sorted by row count, and each carries its issue count and its oldest and newest timestamps.
